INGREDIENTS
The Solid Base:
4 medium potatoes (boiled in their skins and cooled)

3 large eggs (hard-boiled and cooled)

250g (approx. 1/2 lb) cooked ham or doctor's sausage (Doktorskaya kolbasa)

4-5 small radishes

2-3 medium fresh cucumbers

1 large bunch of fresh dill

1 large bunch of fresh green onions (scallions)

Salt and black pepper to taste

The Liquid Base (Choose One):
Option A: 1.5 liters of cold Kvas + 2-3 tablespoons of sour cream (Smetana) for serving

Option B: 1 liter of cold Kefir + 0.5 liters of cold sparkling mineral water

Step-by-Step Instructions
Step 1: Prep the Veggies and Eggs
Peel the boiled potatoes and hard-boiled eggs. Wash the cucumbers, radishes, dill, and green onions, then pat them dry with a paper towel.

Step 2: Dice the Ingredients
Cut the potatoes, eggs, ham (or sausage), cucumbers, and radishes into small, even cubes (about 0.5 cm or 1/4 inch). Place them all into a large pot or mixing bowl.

Step 3: Chop the Herbs
Finely chop the fresh dill and green onions.

Pro Tip: Put the chopped green onions in a small bowl with a pinch of salt and press them gently with a spoon or pestle until they release their juices. This makes the soup much more flavorful. Add the herbs to the large pot.

Step 4: Mix and Season
Mix all the diced ingredients and herbs thoroughly in the pot. Season the mixture with a good pinch of salt and pepper. This is your "Okroshka base."

Step 5: Add the Liquid
If using Kefir: Pour the cold kefir and sparkling mineral water directly into the pot with the ingredients and stir well.

If using Kvas: You can either pour the cold kvas straight into the pot, OR keep the dry mix in the fridge and let everyone put the dry base in their own bowl and pour kvas over it individually.

Step 6: Chill and Serve
Place the soup in the refrigerator for at least 30 minutes to an hour. Okroshka tastes best when it is ice-cold and the flavors have blended together.

Serve with a dollop of sour cream (especially if using the Kvas base) and a slice of rye bread. Enjoy!
